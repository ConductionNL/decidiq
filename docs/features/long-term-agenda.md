# The long-term agenda

The long-term agenda (termijnagenda) is where the council sees what the college
will bring and when. A big topic, such as the energy transition, often reaches
the council along several paths at once, each with its own period, body and
portfolio holder. This page shows how to group those paths as tracks under one
topic, how to find everything of one portfolio holder or one official, and how
to take the list into a committee as a document.

## Split a topic into tracks

A track is an ordinary item on the long-term agenda that names another item as
its topic. It keeps its own subject, body, period and status.

1. Open **Long-term agenda** and open the topic, for example "Energietransitie".
2. In the **Tracks** card, add a track, for example "Warmtevisie 2.0" for
   2026-Q4. The new item is placed under the topic straight away.
3. Add the other tracks the same way.

The topic shows how many tracks it has and how many of them are still open
(planned or shifted). Each track lists its topic in the **Topic** column of the
long-term agenda.

Tracks are one level deep. Decidiq refuses to save:

- a track under an item that is itself a track ("The item was not saved:
  "Warmtevisie 2.0" is itself a track, so it cannot have tracks. Add this track
  to its topic instead.");
- a topic that already has tracks being placed under another topic;
- an item placed under itself.

To see the topics without their tracks, choose the quick filter **Topics only**.

## Find everything of one portfolio holder or one official

The long-term agenda has two columns and two filters for the people behind an
item:

- **Portfolio holder** is the alderman or other office holder who brings the
  item. Pick a name under the **Portfolio holder** filter to list only their
  items.
- **Author** is the official who drafts the proposal (the steller). Pick a name
  under the **Author** filter to see what that official is working on.

Both are people from the register, so a name is recorded once and shows the
same everywhere. The **Owner type** column still says what kind of party owns
an item.

## Download the list as a report

1. Filter the long-term agenda the way you want it, for example
   **Portfolio holder: De Boer** and status **Planned**.
2. Press **Download report**.

Decidiq writes a PDF with exactly the items the filters select that you may
read. Tracks are grouped under their topic, and topics and tracks are ordered
by period. The report is headed with the filters in plain words and today's
date, and lists for each item its subject, body, period, portfolio holder,
author and status.

The PDF is saved in your Files under **Decidiq/Reports** and opens. A report
made again on the same day replaces the earlier one.

- An item you may not read never appears in the report, and neither does the
  name of a topic or a person you may not read.
- When the filters select nothing, Decidiq says "No items match these filters"
  and writes no file.
- The PDF is made by the filinq app. Without filinq the report is saved as an
  HTML page in the same folder, and the page says "filinq is not installed, so
  the report was saved as HTML".

The spreadsheet export of the list is still there, for when you need the data
rather than a document.
